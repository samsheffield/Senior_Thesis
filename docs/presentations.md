# Senior Thesis Milestone 2 Presentations (12/10)

## Overview
Anyone developing a Senior Thesis project is responsible for creating a brief (15 minutes maximum) slideshow presentation which establishes what the project is, the ideas behind it, and what your goals are as designers and artists. You will present this for the class next week.

In addition to looking ahead, this presentation should provide context for what you intend to make, citing your relevant research and prototyping from this semester to demonstrate an understanding of where the work comes from.

__If you are working collaboratively, you are only required to create one presentation as a team.__ 

### Important
- __Please  practice beforehand.__ Make sure you can fit what you want to say/show into the time available.
- __Presentations must be ready when you arrive at class next week.__ No working on the presentation during class.


## Presentation Content
The presentation will establish project goals for your Senior Project and provide some context to your work. This is a guideline for your presentation. You are welcome to expand on this as you feel appropriate but please do not omit any of the talking points below unless they have no direcet connection to the type of work you are making.

### Project Title
__Provide a title for the work you are developing in this class for Senior Thesis.__ You can always change this later if you are feeling indecisive. 

### Who you are
__Provide the names and expected roles of all people contributing to the project.__
- Your name
- Names of team members
- Major/Minor for you and each team member
- What role(s) you are serving in the development of your project. Please try to map this to titles of professional roles in the relevant marketplace.

### Project Description
__Provide a description of your project which succinctly and excitedly describes your work.__ Try to avoid using specific references to other work in your description as a shortcut to describing the experience.

For example, for games... focus on the player experience and avoid talking about tools or technical details in the description unless they are particularly noteworthy. 

Consider...
- What is the key experience you are trying to create?
- What kind of game/film/interactive experience is it? (Genre(s), play style, modality)
- Does it have a theme and setting?
- What is your project's "hook"? (What's one novel, fun, or interesting thing about it?)
- Are there particular novel technologies which you are using?
- How would you describe the overall feeling of your project? (Remember that the words you choose convey different emotional meaning: “walk, stroll, skip, leap, glide, float”, "happy, ecstatic, overjoyed, pleased"). For games, these words chould be _verbs_.


### Precedents

__Provide three significant precedents for your project and briefly describe a detail or two which is inspiring or informative for each.__

What inspired your project? This can be anything which informed the design of your game in some meaningful way. These precedents should be examples which you consider successful at doing something of value relevant to your current project. 


### Artistic and Design Goals
__List and describe three goals for this project you have as a designer.__  

You probably have a lot of things you’d like to accomplish with this project. Prioritize the things you already have some strong feelings on.

Examples of possible design goals include...
- Artistic goals
- Technical or mechanical goals
- Conceptual, thematic, or subject matter-related goals
- Professional development goals

### Experience Goals (For Games and Interactive Art)
__List three specific experience goals for your players/paticipants/users.__ 

These are incredibly important. They’re what you’re designing your project around.  These should be succinct, connect to some emotional experience ([Plutchik’s Wheel of Emotions](https://www.6seconds.org/2022/03/13/plutchik-wheel-emotions/) might be of use), and not be about technical details at all. 

Experience goals need to be as specific as possible. Something like “a fun, but challenging, experience” is too broad. How will you know if your design is successful?  “I want players to feel unexpectedly powerful” or “players will overcome impossible obstacles” is more specific.

Base these on the successes of your prototyping. Look at the things you (and your testers) have found interesting or fun about your proof-of-concept prototype. Identify these unrefined sparks and imagine some experiences you could design which would amplify these successes.

### Documentation of Milestone 2 Prototyping
__Provide documentation of your proof-of-concept prototype/Milestone 2 work.__

Use any type of media that makes sense for your project (images or video) and either embed these or link to them in your presentation. 

This as an opportunity to discuss how the prototype/milestone 2 progress serves as a good basis for your thesis project.

- What’s successful or exciting to you about the prototype? Highlight the strong areas of your prototype.
- What did you learn from the prototype? What do you still need to figure out?
